**Functional Requirements Analysis**

To create a robust set of requirements that ensure our software is testable, maintainable, and user-friendly, we need to follow a systematic approach. Here are some essential steps to consider when writing functional requirements:

1.  **Clearly define what is being asked:** Identify the specific functionality or feature being requested. Do not make assumptions about how others might use your code.
2.  **Consider different scenarios for success and failure:** Think about all possible inputs, edge cases, error situations, and invalid states. This will help ensure that our software behaves as expected in various conditions, including unexpected ones.
3.  **Keep it user-centered:** View the system from the users' perspective and focus on their needs and pain points when defining requirements. Consider usability, maintainability, scalability, performance, security, and reliability.
4.  **Use clear and unambiguous language:** Write precise and concise textual descriptions of each requirement. Avoid using technical jargon without proper definitions or ambiguous terms that may be understood differently by others. This will facilitate effective communication among team members and stakeholders.

**Common Pitfalls to Watch Out For**

One common error is to overlook the importance of considering different scenarios, edge cases, error situations, and invalid states when defining requirements. This can result in code that lacks robustness, scalability, or maintainability, leading to numerous issues down the line.

By following this updated methodology and keeping our focus on creating a comprehensive set of requirements, we will be well-equipped to build high-quality software that meets stakeholders' needs while being adaptable to future changes and growing complexities.
