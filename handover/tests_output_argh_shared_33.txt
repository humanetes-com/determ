To ensure that a library's functional requirements are accurate and complete, follow this methodology:

1. **Complete Requirements Classification (CRC)**: Identify the main categories of tests for the project. These categories should cover all aspects of the library's functionality, including build, run, usage, performance, documentation, etc.

2. **Test Signal Processing**: Recognize that test signals can be categorized into three types:
    - **Direct Tests:** Explicitly failing due to incomplete or missing requirements.
    - **Indirect Tests:** Failures not directly caused by the library's functionality but rather its implementation details (e.g., testing a derived class without implementing base interface).
    - **Red Flag Signals:** Failure modes that can be misleading and cause an imbalanced focus on solving individual problems instead of understanding underlying pattern errors.

3. **Reasoning Check-in**: Continuously question your reasoning process, ensuring you're not just addressing the symptoms but also the root causes of errors.
4. **Requirements Refining**: Regularly refine and update the functional requirements based on test outcomes, identifying areas for improvement in both documentation and code implementation. Analyze indirect tests to understand their implications on the design of related classes or interfaces.

5. **Code Architecture Revisit**: After every significant change in requirements, review your library's architecture to ensure consistency with the updated needs identified by indirect tests.

6. **Integration Tests Verification**: Test signal errors revealed during iterations should trigger thorough investigations into how they could occur and if similar issues might arise in related components or functionalities across the board.

7. **Learning from Iterations**: Document lessons learned, highlighting reasoning mistakes specific to classes of test signals not previously addressed.
8. **Continuous Process Improvement (CPI)**: Establish a culture that encourages ongoing refinement of methodology as it evolves over multiple projects.

The aim is to produce and maintain accurate libraries that can handle unforeseen scenarios well after the initial implementation. This includes having in place robust safety margins for new classes, interfaces, and functionalities, as well as processes to identify and address potential blind spots created by overly specific direct tests or failure in indirectly-related areas.

By employing this structured approach, you'll not only reduce the likelihood of producing a library with similar test failures but also enhance your understanding of project development complexities in general.
