**Understanding Failure Signals**

When writing functional requirements for a project, it's essential to recognize that each failed test reveals a gap in the requirements. These gaps can be categorized into several classes, such as:

* **Absence of a specific behavior or interaction**: The requirements do not account for a particular scenario or interaction between components.
* **Incorrect assumption about a requirement**: A requirement is based on an incorrect assumption about the system's behavior or capabilities.
* **Incomplete description of a requirement**: A requirement lacks crucial details, leading to ambiguity in its implementation.

**Analyzing Failure Signals**

To derive accurate and comprehensive functional requirements, it's necessary to:

1. Identify the specific test that failed, highlighting the symptom (result) of the gap in requirements.
2. Analyze the reasoning process behind the creation of the requirements to determine where the missing or incorrect information originated.
3. Categorize the type of error: absence, incorrect assumption, or incomplete description.

**Closing Gaps with Precise Requirements**

To prevent these errors and ensure thorough requirements, follow a revised methodology:

1. **Requirements Elicitation**: Gather input from stakeholders, domain experts, and potentially affected parties to ensure comprehensive understanding of system behavior.
2. **Explicit Assumptions Check**: Verify all assumptions about the system's capabilities, interactions, or behaviors are explicitly stated and accounted for in requirements.
3. **Clear Requirement Writing**: Use clear language, concise descriptions, and precise syntax while defining each requirement. Ensure that every aspect is covered, without omitting essential details.

**Updated Genesis.md**

By applying this updated methodology, ensure your project's requirements are comprehensive, accurate, and unambiguous, ultimately reducing the likelihood of failed tests related to these errors.

These key takeaways will allow you to build a solid foundation for effective functional requirement writing, eliminating common pitfalls that lead to inadequate or incomplete specifications:

* Identify test failure symptoms to uncover gaps in requirements
* Use explicit assumptions check to prevent incorrect assumptions
* Write clear and concise requirement descriptions to cover all necessary aspects

**Implementation Tips**

- When analyzing failed tests, trace back the reasoning process for each requirement.
- During writing, verify your requirements align with the project's goals and stakeholder expectations.

By implementing this updated approach and following these strategies, you'll greatly minimize opportunities for incomplete or incorrect specifications. This will ensure higher quality requirements, streamlining the development process and ultimately contributing to a successful outcome for your project.
